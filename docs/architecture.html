Merchant Pulse Architecture

High-level design, MLOps loop, detection ensemble, evaluation harness and the nightly run, drawn to walk a recruiter or hiring manager through on a call. Trained on the public M5 Walmart dataset: 3,049 items, 10 stores, 3 states, 5 years of daily unit sales with prices, SNAP days and holiday events.

1 · System design 2 · MLOps loop 3 · Detection ensemble 4 · Eval harness 5 · Nightly run SLOs Likely questions

60-second version. Sales events stream through Pub/Sub and Dataflow into BigQuery. Dataform builds per-series features nightly. Three detectors vote on each series-day, a hierarchical pass decides whether a store-level anomaly is explained by its items, SHAP plus rules name the top three drivers, an uplift model picks the action with the highest expected lift, and Gemini on Vertex AI writes the three-sentence merchant note. Every model goes through an eval gate on injected anomalies before Vertex AI Model Registry will promote it, canaries on Cloud Run, and rolls back in under five minutes. Merchant confirm/dismiss clicks feed back as labels.

1 · System design

Left to right: where sales data enters, where it rests, what reasons over it, and who acts on the result. The dashed boundary is one GCP project.

Say this

"Two ingestion paths, one landing zone. Point-of-sale events stream through Pub/Sub and Dataflow so an intraday spike is visible within minutes. Prices, promos and inventory arrive daily through a scheduled BigQuery MERGE. Both write to one partitioned raw table."

"Dataform builds the feature table: lags, rolling z-scores, price deltas, SNAP and holiday flags. Every model reads that table, so a feature bug is fixed in one place."

"The intelligence column is a chain: detect, reconcile up the hierarchy, attribute drivers, recommend an action, write the note. The output is one alert row a merchant can act on, not a score."

"The orange loop at the bottom is the part most teams skip. Confirm or dismiss on an alert becomes a label for the next training run."

If he asks

  • Why BigQuery and not a time-series DB? Merchants and analysts already query in SQL. Daily cadence fits partitioned tables. Streaming insert covers the intraday case.
  • Why Dataflow if the model runs nightly? Dedupe, late-arriving data and exactly-once semantics are solved once at ingest. Scoring cadence can change without touching ingestion.
  • What does the merchant actually see? Store, item or category, anomaly type, severity, three drivers, one recommended action, confidence, and a three-sentence note. Plus an "ask" box that runs Text2SQL over the same tables.

2 · MLOps loop

Clockwise: code enters at the top left, a trained candidate must pass an eval gate to be registered, deploys through a canary, is monitored in production, and the monitor decides when to retrain. The orange dashed path is the rollback.

Say this

"Code goes through Cloud Build, which compiles and submits the Vertex AI pipeline. The pipeline validates the data first. If yesterday's volume is off by more than two sigma from the 28-day mean, it stops before training on bad data."

"The gate is the key control. The eval harness injects labeled anomalies into a holdout and the candidate has to beat an F1 of 0.85, stay under five false alerts per thousand series-days, and detect within two days. Fail means no registry entry and a notification."

"Canary is 10% of traffic for 48 hours in shadow mode, scored side by side with the current version. Then full rollout with a latency SLO."

"Monitoring watches three things: feature drift, alert volume per store, and precision computed from merchant confirm/dismiss. Any of them crossing a threshold triggers retraining or, if it is sudden, a rollback."

If he asks

  • How fast is rollback? Under five minutes. Cloud Run keeps previous revisions; rollback is a traffic split change, not a rebuild.
  • How do you know precision in production without labels? Merchant feedback is the label. Confirm counts as a true positive, dismiss as a false positive. Weekly sample of suppressed alerts is reviewed to estimate recall.
  • How often does it retrain? Weekly on schedule, or earlier when drift on the top features crosses a threshold. Prophet models refit weekly and only predict daily, which keeps nightly compute small.
  • What is in lineage? Data snapshot, feature SQL commit, hyperparameters, eval report, who approved. Every alert row carries the model version that produced it.

3 · Detection ensemble

Why three detectors and a vote instead of one model: each detector covers a failure mode the others miss, and requiring agreement is what keeps the false-alert rate inside budget.

Say this

"The residual detector catches what the forecast did not expect: a drop against trend, a holiday that did not lift. Isolation Forest catches combinations that are individually normal but jointly odd, like a price cut with no volume response. The autoencoder catches shape changes over a 28-day window, like a slow bleed."

"Each threshold is set on holdout to a false-alert budget, then the vote on top. One detector firing is logged, not alerted."

"Reconciliation stops the alert storm. If a store-level drop is fully explained by three items being out of stock, the merchant gets one alert about three items, not forty alerts."

"Attribution is SHAP on the Isolation Forest plus a rule layer for the things SHAP cannot name: same-day price change, SNAP day, holiday, or a zero-sales streak that is almost always a stockout."

If he asks

  • Why not one model? Each detector has a blind spot. Residual models miss multivariate oddities; Isolation Forest ignores time order; autoencoders are slow to react to sharp spikes. Voting trades a little recall for a lot of precision, which is what merchants need.
  • Cold start for new items? Fall back to the category-level model until the item has eight weeks of history. Reconciliation already works at that level.
  • Severity? Revenue at risk: anomaly magnitude × price × expected days to resolve. Alerts are ranked by it and capped per merchant per day.
  • Compute cost? About 30k series. Prophet refits weekly, predicts daily. Isolation Forest and the autoencoder score in one Cloud Run job with eight parallel tasks, well under 20 minutes.

4 · Evaluation harness

Two tracks. The top one answers "does it find real anomalies without crying wolf". The bottom one answers "do the recommendations actually move sales". Both feed the pipeline gate in figure 2.

Say this

"Real anomaly labels are rare, so I manufacture them. Take the last 90 days for a fifth of the stores, inject five anomaly types with known start and end, run the ensemble with production thresholds, and score it. That gives precision, recall and F1 per anomaly type, false alerts per thousand series-days, and median days to detect."

"The second track is the one that matters to a merchant. M5 has five years of real price changes and promo events. For each event, the uplift model predicts lift, I match a control series in the same category with no event, and compare realized to predicted. Sign accuracy and lift error are the metrics."

"Both reports are produced by the pipeline and attached to the model version. The gate reads them."

If he asks

  • Aren't injected anomalies easier than real ones? Yes, so they are a floor, not a ceiling. Production precision comes from merchant feedback and is tracked separately in monitoring.
  • How do you measure recall in production? Weekly stratified sample of suppressed series reviewed by hand; estimated recall with a confidence interval goes into the monitoring dashboard.
  • Why matched control and not an A/B test? Historical data has no randomization. Matched control is the honest offline estimate. In production, recommendations would ship with a holdout group of merchants for a true A/B read.

5 · Nightly run

What happens between two and three in the morning, so a merchant opens a finished digest at eight.

Say this

"The whole nightly run is an hour. Scheduler kicks it off, features build in BigQuery, a Cloud Run job scores everything in parallel, attribution and uplift write the alert rows, Gemini writes the notes, and Looker refreshes before anyone is at a desk."

"Every step is keyed on run date, so a retry overwrites instead of duplicating. If the Gemini step fails, merchants still get the alert with drivers and action; the note says 'narrative pending'."

If he asks

  • Intraday? The streaming path keeps raw current. An hourly lightweight job runs only the residual detector on the top 500 revenue series for same-day spikes.
  • Cost at this scale? BigQuery stays inside the free tier, Cloud Run scales to zero, Vertex pipeline runs are a few dollars each. Under $50 a month at M5 scale on the project budget alert.

Likely questions, short answers

Why GCP for this?

BigQuery is the natural home for retail-scale daily sales, Dataform keeps feature SQL versioned, Vertex AI gives pipelines, registry and monitoring in one place, Cloud Run scales to zero between nightly runs, and Gemini sits inside the same IAM boundary as the data.

How does this scale from M5 to a real marketplace?

Same code. Partition by store and date, Dataflow autoscales ingestion, scoring moves from a Cloud Run job to Vertex AI batch prediction, Prophet refits move to a weekly Dataproc job. Alert caps and reconciliation are what keep output manageable, not compute.

What would you cut first under time pressure?

The autoencoder. Residual plus Isolation Forest with a two-of-two vote gets most of the precision. The eval harness and rollback never get cut.

Where does GenAI actually add value here?

Two places only. Writing the merchant note from structured drivers so it is grounded, and Text2SQL for follow-up questions over the same tables. Detection stays classical because it has to be calibrated and cheap.

How do you handle seasonality vs. anomaly?

Prophet and SARIMAX model yearly and weekly seasonality plus holiday and SNAP regressors, so a Thanksgiving spike is expected, not anomalous. The anomaly is deviation from that expectation.

What is the first thing you would ship?

Residual detector, eval harness, alerts table, Looker view. One week. An honest precision number on day seven beats a full ensemble on day thirty.
